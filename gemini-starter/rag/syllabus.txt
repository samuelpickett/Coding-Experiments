Learning Outcomes: The learning outcomes found in the course catalog are listed below.
Objectives:
1. Gain proficiency in solving systems of linear equations using matrices, and demonstrate a working
knowledge of the algebraic properties of matrices.
2. Understand Euclidean vector spaces, their inherent arithmetic, and algebraic structure, and the
accompanying geometry that arises.
3. Acquire facility working with general vector spaces, linear transformations, coordinate vectors,
and changing of bases.
4. Develop an algebraic and geometric understanding of eigenvalues, eigenvectors, and eigenspaces.
5. Use mathematical software and/or calculators to solve a variety of applications in the Physical
Sciences, Computer Science, or Economics.
Written Homework: Homework exercises for most sections will be assigned and turned in through
Canvas. See the posted class schedule for due dates and problems assigned. Written homework
allows you to develop your mathematical writing and communication skills. Organized, neat work is
expected.
There is 24-hour grace period in which late homework is not penalized. After the grace period
expires, late homework will receive a 10% deduction per day up to 30%. You will always be entitled
to receive at least 70% of the assignment point total no matter how late you turn it in. For example,
if you complete your score report for an assignment 50 hours after it was due and you scored 46/50
points, you would receive 40/50 points.
There are three parts to completing a homework assignment. The first part is to complete the
problem set. The second part is to convert your problem set to a pdf and upload it in Canvas. The
third part is to grade your homework using the provided solutions. The solutions are available in
Canvas once you have uploaded your problem set.
Every homework assignment is worth five times the number of problems listed in the problem
set. In other words, each problem is worth five points. In general, a complete and correct solution
is worth five points. Very small errors such as dropping a negative sign or a row reduction error
would be a half point deduction. If there were more than one such error, you should give yourself
a four. If you gave an honest attempt at solving the problem, but didn’t have a correct approach
or interpretation, you should get three points out of the five. Attempting a problem would mean
writing down your thoughts about the problem, writing down which theorem or theorems might
apply, attempting some calculations, and/or interpreting those calculations. Don’t focus too much
on errors in your computations. We all make mistakes when doing computations by hand. Did
you understand the process? Did you understand why you were row reducing or performing the
calculations? Did you interpret the calculations correctly? Often there are True/False questions in
the homework. These are worth five points as well. Deduct one half point for each incorrect part.
Please justify your answer with a theorem or with text from the book. Answering a True/ False
question correctly includes providing a justification. In summary,
• 5 points for a complete and correct solution,
• 4.5 points for a solution with one small error such as a sign error or row reduction error,
• 4 points for a solution with more than one small error,
• 3 points for a reasonable attempt at the problem (i.e. you explained your thoughts, cited
theorems, or performed some calculations but you were not approaching the problem correctly),
• 0 points for leaving a question blank.
Each assignment has a score report quiz in Canvas. In each score report, you will provide your
score as well as a list of the problems for which you deducted points. You should include the problem
number, the point deduction, and a brief explanation of your mistake.

Reading Quizzes: Reading quizzes are part of preparing for class. Almost every class period will
have a reading quiz due 15 minutes before class. (No reading quizzes on review days.) You can access
the reading quizzes in Canvas. I expect you to read the theorems in each section and spend time
trying to understand them. I do not expect you to understand everything that you read. Reading
mathematical texts is a skill. It takes time, effort, and practice to develop that skill. Focus on
understanding definitions and theorems. If you have time, go through examples and try to make up
your own examples.
The reading quizzes are not timed. Some students open the reading quiz first to be able to see
the questions before reading through the theorems.
Generally, reading quizzes consist of three questions and are open book. The first question asks
whether you spent at least 15 minutes studying the section. This includes time spent on taking the
quiz. The next question is meant to focus your attention on important concepts, ideas, and theorems.
The last question is a multiple choice question. As the reading quizzes are preparation assignments,
late quizzes have a maximum score of 7 out of 10 points. Your three lowest reading quiz scores will
be dropped.

Exams: There are three exams and a final exam. Each exam has a testing center portion and a
take-home portion. For the testing center portion, you will not be allowed any resources other than a
scientific calculator and a 3 inch by 5 inch notecard. For the take-home portion, you will be allowed
to use your book and computational technology such as Python or Mathematica.
For each of the three exams in the testing center, there will be an opportunity to retake a similar
exam about a week after each regular exam closes. These retake exams are optional. The better of
the original exam score and the retake exam score will be used.
For each of the three take-home exams, you will have one opportunity to correct and resubmit
after they have been graded.

The Learning Model The Learning Model which is based on the three steps Prepare, Teach One
Another, and Ponder and Prove, is a powerful way to learn and will be used in this course. Prepare:
By completing the homework assigned the previous class period before the next class period, you will
be prepared for the next material. You should also read the text book section prior to our discussion
on it in class. Teach One Another: I encourage you to be engaged in class and an active participant
in teaching and learning with your fellow class mates. This can happen both in and out side of class
time. Ponder and Prove: The selected homework assignments provide the opportunity to solidify the
concepts learned. I also encourage you to utilize the five principles of the learning model: Exercise
Faith, Learn by the Holy Ghost, Lay Hold on the Word of God, Act for Themselves, and Love, Serve,
and Teach One Another.
